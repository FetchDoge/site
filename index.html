Chapter I — The thesis

Agents you can hold to account.

Autonomous systems fail in three predictable ways: they exceed their authority, they burn budget nobody measured, and nobody can reconstruct what they did. My work attacks all three — with runtime controls, token governance, and ledgers that stamp every claim with its provenance.

Pillar 01

Safe

Bounded authority at runtime: leases that expire, an e-stop that actually stops, a fault bus that records. Interlock and Hermes treat an agent's power as a loan, not a possession.

Pillar 02

Economical

Tokens are finite inventory. AEGIS packs context once, reuses it while the bytes are unchanged, and holds a protective reserve floor — with an honest-yield rule that keeps savings claims null until an admitted pair of runs proves them.

Pillar 03

Auditable

Every consequential claim carries its status — verified, inferred, guessed, unknown. Ledgers, receipts, and evidence packets make the work reconstructible by someone who was never in the room.

Agent safety substrate

A Python runtime substrate that makes agent authority bounded by construction. Leases expire. The e-stop halts. The fault bus records what happened and who held the lease when it did. Claims, not vibes.

Chapter III — Method

Every claim carries its status.

The Ektar standard: the finest grain, no fog. Nothing on this site is louder than its evidence. Consequential claims are tiered at the point of writing — and the tiers are part of the design, not the fine print.

Verified An executed trace exists — a release tag, a green CI run, a published ledger entry. Cited, dated, checkable. ●
Inferred Follows from verified material by a stated chain of reasoning. The chain is shown, not assumed. ◐
Guessed A working hypothesis, labeled as one. Guesses are allowed; unlabeled guesses are not. ○
Unknown Explicitly not known. An unmarked gap becomes a lie by the next reader — so the gaps are marked. ?
